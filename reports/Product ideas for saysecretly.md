# Turn saysecretly.com into secret surprises, not confessions

Build a **"collect messages secretly, reveal on the day" group card**: one organizer invites friends or coworkers to add notes for a birthday, farewell, retirement or wedding, and the recipient sees the finished card only at a set reveal time. It is the best fit for saysecretly.com because it is the only idea in the evidence that has all four of these: people already pay for it, demand runs all year, every card spreads the product, and the legal exposure is low. The incumbent Kudoboard charges **$6.99–$99 per board and $299–$449 a year for teams** ([Kudoboard](https://www.kudoboard.com/pricing)). Tribute.co, a small group-video company, has estimated revenue of about **$3.2M in 2024** ([GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/tributeco)), though that is a third-party estimate. The original Secretly (anonymous confessions, Q&A, secret exchange, an 18+ section) failed for the same reasons the whole category has failed since 2014. Strangers form weak ties, Reddit and AI chatbots already meet the "get it off my chest" need for free, and the business model that worked, charging people to learn who sent a message, has drawn FTC action twice since 2024. The second-best option is an **anonymous staff feedback and reporting box for small US frontline businesses**. Laws and compliance pressure create real demand there and buyers pay monthly, but it needs B2B selling and has a much higher bar for trust and security. Three other ideas round out the shortlist: time-locked "say it later" messages, an office Secret Santa or White Elephant add-on, and an 18+ anonymous-note or mutual-crush hook. Each works better as a feature or seasonal traffic source than as the core business.

## The old Secretly failed for reasons the category has repeated since 2014

There is plenty of demand for anonymous sharing in the US, but it goes to venues that already have an audience or a specific job. General confession sites get little of it. On Reddit, r/AmItheAsshole has about **24.4M members** ([Hive Index](https://thehiveindex.com/communities/r-amitheasshole/)) and r/relationship_advice about 15.8M ([GummySearch](https://gummysearch.com/r/AmItheAsshole/)), while r/confessions has about **1.3M** ([GummySearch](https://gummysearch.com/r/confessions/)). People want a verdict or advice on their situation much more than they want to confess into the void. Those member counts are third-party snapshots, because Reddit stopped showing public member counts in September 2025 ([Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/reddit-member-count-new-engagement-metrics-10067678)). One 10.6M figure for r/confession is probably a tracking error. The anonymous products that broke out each attached to an existing context. NGL rode Instagram bio links ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/07/11/anonymous-social-ngl-tops-15m-installs-2-4m-in-revenue-as-users-complain-about-being-scammed/embed/)). Fizz is campus-only and reports 1M+ users on 800+ campuses ([Simplify/BI repost](https://simplify.jobs/p/a28c2795-06e1-4c37-a5bd-e28bfd480307/Product-Research-Intern)). Tea, where women warn each other about men they are dating, reached **#1 on the US App Store in July 2025** and then leaked about 72k images, including ID selfies ([ABC7/AP](https://abc7news.com/post/tea-app-was-intended-help-women-date-safely-got-hacked/17332900/)).

AI is now a direct competitor for private venting. In a September 2025 survey, **32% of US adults** said they had told an AI something they had not told the people close to them in the past 30 days. The sample was small (n=353, ±6.8%) ([Verasight](https://data.verasight.io/ai/a-third-have-told-an-ai-something-they-havent-told-the-people-close-to-them)). YouGov puts the figure at 13% of all adults and 23% of under-30s ([TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/ai-chatbots-relationships-emotional-support-sycophancy)). A confession site is competing with a free, always-available listener that replies instantly.

The history of these apps follows one pattern. **Secret** reported 15M+ users and raised about $35M, then closed after 16 months ([CNN Money](https://money.cnn.com/2015/04/29/technology/secret-closes-investors/)). Its founder said the mainstream "will never embrace" anonymity, and of 25 anonymous apps listed in 2014, all but 3 were inactive by 2016 ([Valley News/Washington Post](https://vnews.com/2016/09/30/these-apps-were-made-to-share-your-secrets-and-that-s-why-they-couldn-t-last-5096560/)). A study of Whisper found users formed only "weak ties" with strangers, so nothing brought them back (same source). Whisper later left about 900M records exposed ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2020-03-11-whisper-open-database-exposed-users.html)). **Gas** reached 1M daily users within two months in 2022, peaked within three months, and Discord shut it down in November 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/discord-kills-gas-anonymous-compliments-app-bought-nine-months-ago)). Facebook closed **tbh** within nine months of buying it ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/07/02/facebook-is-shutting-down-hello-moves-and-the-anonymous-teen-app-tbh-due-to-low-usage)). Apple and Google pulled **Sarahah** in 2018 after a petition with about 470K signatures ([Change.org](https://www.change.org/p/12898804/u/22412492)). **Yik Yak** fell from No. 3 to No. 447 on the App Store amid bullying and threats ([Cornell INFO 2040](https://blogs.cornell.edu/info2040/2016/11/10/yik-yak-and-the-network-effect/)). Secretly had the same weaknesses: no existing social graph to grow through, strangers with only weak ties, several unrelated jobs bundled together, free incumbents, and an 18+ section that raised moderation costs. The durable anonymous products share three traits: a bounded, verified audience; a reason to come back beyond curiosity; and revenue that does not depend on revealing who sent a message ([Mobile Dev Memo](https://mobiledevmemo.com/the-importance-of-facebook-shuttering-tbh-9-months-after-acquiring-it/)).

## Regulators have shut down the profitable "who sent this?" model

The proven way to make money from anonymous messaging was a weekly subscription that promised to reveal the sender, and regulators have now gone after it. NGL earned about **$2.4M within months** of its 2022 launch by selling $9.99-a-week "hints" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/07/11/anonymous-social-ngl-tops-15m-installs-2-4m-in-revenue-as-users-complain-about-being-scammed/embed/)). Undated Sensor Tower pages still show it at hundreds of thousands of dollars a month on US iOS ([Sensor Tower](https://app.sensortower.com/overview/1596550932?country=US)). A claim that NGL has made $100M+ in total comes from one unverified blog. In July 2024 the FTC and the LA County DA settled with NGL for **$5M and the first US order barring a platform from serving anyone under 18**. NGL had to add an age gate, stop misrepresenting senders and AI moderation, and get express consent for recurring charges ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/ftc-bans-anonymous-messaging-app-ngl-for-minors-rcna160897)). In September 2025 the FTC and DOJ sued **Sendit** and its CEO personally over similar conduct: fake provocative messages, a $9.99 "Diamond" reveal that did not actually reveal, undisclosed weekly billing, and data on more than **116,000 self-declared under-13 users** ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-alleges-sendit-app-its-ceo-unlawfully-collected-personal-data-children-deceived-users-about)). Founders were named in both cases.

Other rules narrow the space further. The amended COPPA Rule took effect June 23, 2025, with full compliance due **April 22, 2026** ([Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/coppas-amended-rule-is-now-in-full-effect-what-operators-need-to-know.html)). In October 2026 the Eleventh Circuit allowed Florida's HB 3 limits on minors' social media accounts ([Florida Phoenix](https://floridaphoenix.com/2026/10/14/appeals-court-lifts-block-on-florida-law-restricting-social-media-access-for-teens/)), while several other states' laws are still blocked ([WABE](https://www.wabe.org/judge-blocks-georgias-social-media-age-verification-law-citing-free-speech-concerns/)). Section 230 still protects hosting what users post, but it is no shield for an app's own promises. In *Bride v. YOLO* (9th Cir. 2024), claims based on YOLO's promise to unmask and ban harassers were allowed to proceed ([Eric Goldman](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2024/08/when-it-comes-to-section-230-the-ninth-circuit-is-a-chaos-agent-estate-of-bride-v-yolo.htm)). In *Lemmon v. Snap*, claims about an app's own product design were allowed to proceed ([Covington](https://www.insideprivacy.com/data/ninth-circuit-denies-section-230-defense-in-products-liability-case/)). Apple's Guideline 1.2 says apps "used primarily for… random or anonymous chat" do not belong on the App Store, and 1.1.6 rejects anonymous SMS apps ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Snap has banned anonymous messaging through its developer platform since March 2022 ([Snap](https://values.snap.com/news/announcing-new-policies-for-snaps-developer-platform)). In practice, any consumer anonymity product on saysecretly.com should be web-first and 18+ with a real age gate. It should never send synthetic messages, never sell unmasking, make only safety claims it actually enforces, and make cancellation one click. Workplace reporting is the one area where the law creates demand rather than risk. SOX §301 requires public-company audit committees to accept anonymous complaints ([Perkins Coie](https://www.perkinscoie.com/en/news-insights/whistleblower-provisions-of-the-sarbanes-oxley-act-some.html)). The US Sentencing Guidelines and DOJ's compliance guidance reward anonymous or confidential reporting channels ([USSC comment](https://www.ussc.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/amendment-process/public-hearings-and-meetings/20040317-19/ERCFellowscomment.pdf); [NAVEX](https://www.navex.com/blog/article/how-to-get-the-most-out-of-your-confidential-hotline/)). California's SB 553 requires confidential reporting procedures at most employers with 10+ staff, though sources disagree on whether anonymity itself is required ([Clym](https://regulations.clym.io/california-senate-bill-no-553); [Spot](https://talktospot.com/sb553)).

## Businesses and groups pay when secrecy does a specific job

Where money changes hands, people are paying for secrecy that does a specific job: keeping a surprise, or protecting a reporter. Paying for secrecy itself is not where the money is. Group cards and tributes are the clearest consumer and team market. **Kudoboard** sells boards for $6.99, $9.99 and $19.99, a $99 memorial board, and team plans at $299–$449 a year, with logos from T-Mobile, Gusto and Airbnb ([Kudoboard](https://www.kudoboard.com/pricing)). Average spend is about $126 a year for small businesses, according to SpendHound's unverified self-reported data ([SpendHound](https://www.spendhound.com/marketplace/kudoboard-pricing)). **Tribute.co** charges about $29.99–$99 per group video. GetLatka estimates its revenue at $3.2M and calls it bootstrapped, but CB Insights lists $4M raised, so treat both as unverified ([GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/tributeco); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/tributeco)). **FutureMe** is the cleanest solo-founder precedent with a secrecy theme. One founder ran it on about **$500 a month** in costs for 19 years and sold it in 2021 for a reported "mid-7 figures" ([TheyGotAcquired](https://theygotacquired.com/software/futureme-acquired-by-memories-group/)).

Secret Santa is large but seasonal and free at the consumer level. DrawNames says about 35M names were drawn in 2024 ([DrawNames](https://www.drawnames.com:443)), and Elfster claimed 17M+ users a year in 2021, earning from retail affiliate links rather than user fees ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/retailers/how-elfster-is-capitalizing-on-digital-gifting-to-entice-retail-brand-partners/)). US searches for "secret santa gifts" rise about **10x from October to December** (keyword-tool estimate) ([EcomCrew](https://products.ecomcrew.com/search-term/secret%20santa%20gifts)). Companies do pay for virtual White Elephant events: about $69–$299 for self-serve software ([SoftwareFinder](https://softwarefinder.com/hr/white-elephant-online)) and $552–$1,550+ for hosted events ([CourseHorse](https://coursehorse.com/groups/virtual/event/online-white-elephant-gift-exchange)).

Anonymous workplace feedback is the larger, slower B2B market. Analyst estimates disagree so widely that they are weak evidence. Whistleblowing software is put anywhere from **$0.27B to $2.73B** ([Coherent via GII](https://www.giiresearch.com/report/coh2084756-whistleblowing-software-market-by-deployment-type.html); [WiseGuy](https://www.wiseguyreports.com/reports/whistleblowing-software-market)) and employee engagement software at about $0.8–1.1B ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/employee-engagement-software-market-statistics)). None of the estimates is US-only. The price points are more useful. Enterprise ethics hotlines such as NAVEX reportedly start at $5–10K a year plus setup fees, according to VoxWel, a competitor ([VoxWel](https://voxwel.com/blogs/whistleblowing-software-pricing-comparison)). Small-business hotlines charge flat fees: **Safe Hotline $600 a year** ([Safe Hotline](https://safehotline.com/)) and FraudHL $500 a year ([FraudHL](https://www.fraudhl.com/)). **Incogneato** sells anonymous suggestion boxes with chat for **$49–$99 a month** ([Incogneato](https://www.incognea.to/)). The main buyer complaint is trust: employees doubt that "anonymous" really means anonymous ([CultureMonkey](https://www.culturemonkey.io/guides/anonymous-employee-feedback/open-anonymous-feedback/pros-and-cons-of-anonymous-employee-feedback/)). NAVEX benchmarks show why small firms buy for compliance rather than daily use. US organizations get a median of **1.78 reports per 100 employees per year**, about half of them anonymous, and web intake now exceeds phone ([NAVEX](https://cdn.navex.com/image/upload/v1748609318/resources-2025/benchmarking-reports/navex-2025-regional-bmk-whistleblowing-key-findings_US_EN.pdf)). A 50-person restaurant group might receive one report a year. No revenue figure was found for any small vendor in this space.

## Five ideas ranked for a solo Next.js and Supabase founder

The ranking weighs four things: evidence that people pay, whether demand lasts all year, whether the product spreads through its own use, and whether one non-specialist builder can ship and run it safely. Difficulty assumes Next.js, Supabase (auth, Postgres, storage, scheduled jobs), Stripe Checkout and a transactional email service.

| Rank | Idea | Target customer | Demand evidence | Main competitors | Monetization | Solo difficulty | Key risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Secret group card with timed reveal** | Friends and families (birthdays, weddings, memorials); team leads and HR (farewells, work anniversaries) | Kudoboard's paid tiers and logos; Tribute est. $3M+ | Kudoboard, GroupGreeting, Tribute, Punchbowl Memento | $5–15 per card unlock; $19–39/mo team plan | **Low–medium** | Crowded; must win on simplicity, keeping the surprise, and price; gift-money pooling adds payment/KYC work, so defer it |
| 2 | **Anonymous staff feedback and reporting box** for 10–250-person frontline businesses | Restaurants, retail, clinics, franchises, nonprofits; California SB 553 is a hook | Flat-fee vendors exist at $500–1,200/yr; legal drivers (SOX, Sentencing Guidelines, SB 553, FAR) | Incogneato, Safe Hotline, FraudHL, FaceUp, AllVoices, free QR forms | Flat $29–99/mo per location | **Medium–high** | B2B sales; one breach ends the brand; handling harassment reports; low daily use leads to churn |
| 3 | **"Say it later" time-locked messages** to others or a group | Parents (letter at 18), wedding guests, graduates, couples | FutureMe solo exit; evergreen with New Year and graduation spikes | FutureMe, Tribute (for video) | Small per-message or annual fee; group capsules per event | **Low** | Weak pricing power; slow SEO; must reliably deliver years later |
| 4 | **Office Secret Santa / White Elephant** | Remote and hybrid teams, HR | 10x Dec search spike; $69–299 paid event software | Elfster, DrawNames (free), White Elephant Online, hosted-event firms | $49–199 per event | **Low** (drawing); **high** if it handles shipping | About 6–8 weeks of revenue a year; free consumer incumbents dominate search |
| 5 | **18+ anonymous honest notes and mutual-crush matcher** | Adults under 30, college students around Valentine's Day | Gas 1M DAU; recurring crush apps; Reddit advice volume | NGL (now 18+), Mutual, Fizz, AI chatbots | Sender-paid extras only (never unmasking) | **Medium**, with ongoing moderation | FTC and COPPA history, Apple 1.2, bullying, TCPA for contact outreach, rapid novelty fade |

The **group card** ranks first because its weaknesses are manageable and it fixes the original site's core flaw. Every card brings 10–30 contributors and one recipient, and each of them sees saysecretly.com at a moment of goodwill. This is the same spread-through-use effect NGL got from bio links, without the abuse. The brand fits directly: "everyone says something, secretly, until the reveal." Contributors are invited by an organizer who knows the recipient, so content moderation stays light. The steelman for ranking it lower is that Kudoboard is entrenched and well liked, so a newcomer must win on niche positioning (surprise-keeping, a scheduled reveal, an optional anonymous note) and on search for specific occasions.

The **feedback box** has the best long-term revenue: monthly payments, compliance-driven buyers, and transparent pricing as a differentiator against demo-gated competitors. If the founder enjoys selling to businesses, it could be the better company. It ranks second because the product's whole value is trust. It needs provable anonymity (no IP logging, metadata stripping, delayed delivery so timing cannot identify the sender), two-way anonymous chat (now standard, as at [FaceUp](https://www.faceup.com/en/pricing)), and secure case logs. Getting that wrong is far costlier than a broken greeting card, and small businesses do not find vendors through viral sharing. One study also found that adding an anonymous channel can reduce people's intention to report through any channel ([Monash](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/an-examination-of-the-effects-of-procedural-safeguards-on-intenti/)), which complicates the sales story.

Ideas three and four work best as features of the winner rather than separate businesses. A time-locked card is simply a card with a far-off reveal date. A Secret Santa drawer is an obvious way to bring in traffic each November. Idea five should exist, if at all, only as a February traffic campaign: "tell us your crush; we only tell you both if it's mutual," 18+, with no fake messages and no paid reveals.

## Recommendation: ship a surprise card in four weeks and let Q4 test it

Build **Say Secretly Cards** on the existing Next.js and Supabase codebase and retire the confessions, Q&A and After Dark features, which carry moderation and brand-safety costs without traction. The MVP has five pieces. First, an organizer creates a card with the recipient's name, the occasion, a reveal date and time, and an optional organizer email. Second, the organizer gets a contributor link and a private admin link. Neither requires a contributor account, which keeps sign-up friction near zero. Third, contributors add a text message with an optional photo or GIF and choose to sign their name or post as "Anonymous." The site states plainly what anonymous means, for example that the organizer and the recipient cannot see who posted it, and only claims what the database actually enforces. Fourth, a Supabase scheduled job emails the recipient a reveal link at the set time, and that link opens a full-screen, swipeable card. This reuses the existing mobile card UI. Fifth, the card is free up to a small cap, such as 10 messages. A one-time Stripe Checkout payment of about $9 unlocks unlimited messages, a downloadable PDF keepsake and no branding. There are no subscriptions at launch, so auto-renew rules do not apply. The organizer moderation tools needed are simple: hide or delete a message, and a report link.

Keep these out of version one: video, gift-money pooling (Stripe Connect and KYC), a native app, AI writing help, and team plans. Add a **$19–29 a month team plan** only after several paying organizers ask for repeat office use. Distribution should come from the product itself. Use a "Make your own surprise card" footer on every reveal, and add search landing pages for specific occasions (farewell card for a coworker, retirement card, group birthday card for a teacher). With Q4 starting now, add an office Secret Santa name-drawer and a holiday team card in November as seasonal hooks. A reasonable, unsourced test: if about 50 paid unlocks do not come in by the end of January, the price or the positioning is wrong. In that case, try the feedback-box idea against California frontline employers, reusing the same anonymous-posting core.

## Which numbers to trust

Few hard 2025–2026 figures exist in this category, so the recommendation rests on prices and documented events rather than market sizes. The most reliable evidence is the published prices (Kudoboard, Safe Hotline, FraudHL, Incogneato), the FTC actions and court rulings, NAVEX's benchmark data, and dated shutdowns. The weakest evidence is analyst market sizes, which differ by about 10x for whistleblowing software; Tribute's revenue, which is GetLatka's estimate and conflicts with CB Insights; Kudoboard's "millions of people" and Elfster's 17M users, both self-reported and old; NGL's $100M+ total, from one unverified blog; Fizz's $200M valuation cap, reported second-hand through a job listing; and competitor price estimates published by VoxWel, itself a competitor. No 2025–2026 download or revenue data was found for NGL, Sendit or Tellonym. There are also no TikTok hashtag totals, no retention figures for anonymous apps, and no revenue data for any indie feedback or card tool besides FutureMe's exit range. The $9 unlock and $19–29 team-plan prices, and the 50-unlock test, are extrapolations from competitor prices. No source validates them.

## Conclusion

The original Secretly offered anonymity itself as the product, and the evidence shows that does not work. Anonymity sells when it serves a specific outcome between people who already know each other: a surprise kept until the right moment, or a concern raised without fear of retaliation. That points saysecretly.com toward organizers who already pay $7–$30 per occasion, and away from strangers who leave once the novelty wears off. It also changes what "secretly" promises users, from hiding who you are to keeping the surprise until the reveal, which regulators have no reason to police.

The card business can also lead to the B2B option. The same features (invite links, anonymous posting with honest guarantees, scheduled delivery, organizer moderation) are most of what an anonymous staff feedback box needs. Starting with cards lets a solo founder build trust in the brand and test payments on low-stakes greeting cards before taking on workplace reports, where a mistake is costly.
