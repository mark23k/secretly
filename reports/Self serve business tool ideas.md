# Build a better Shopify label printer first

**The 30-second answer.** Build a small add-on for Shopify stores that prints price tags, shelf labels and barcode stickers correctly on the label sheets US shops already buy (Avery, Dymo). The people who pay are owners of physical-product shops: boutiques, wineries, chocolate makers, gift shops. They find it by typing "barcode labels" or "price labels" into the Shopify App Store, the built-in store where shops find add-ons, so you never have to contact anyone. Shopify charges them on their normal Shopify bill and, under current rules, takes 0% of your first $1 million. A fair price is a free plan for light use and about $10–20 a month for full use. Plan on 6–8 weeks to build, a few months of earning under $500 a month while reviews build up, roughly $1,000 a month somewhere around 9–15 months after launch, and a possible $3,000–$10,000 a month after 2–3 years if it works. That is a steady side income, not a fortune. The reason to pick this one is that **Shopify's own free label app is rated 2.3 out of 5 from 476 reviews**, and shop owners were still posting complaints about it in June and July 2026 ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/retail-barcode-labels/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1&ratings%5B%5D=2&sort_by=newest)). If, by April 30, 2027, the app has fewer than 20 installs, stop and reuse the same Shopify groundwork for the second-ranked idea, a wholesale catalog maker.

A few words used throughout, in plain terms. **Monthly income** means money from subscriptions that renews every month (often written "MRR"). **App marketplace** means a store inside a business platform, such as the Shopify App Store or the Google Workspace Marketplace, where businesses search for add-ons. **Reviews** on these stores are the best public stand-in for how many businesses use an app, because the stores do not show install counts (Google's store does show them). **"Built for Shopify" badge** is a quality badge Shopify gives apps that meet its speed and design rules and have at least 50 installs and 5 reviews ([Shopify docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/built-for-shopify/requirements)).

## App marketplaces beat Google search for a founder who will not sell

The research looked at three ways a business could find the product without being contacted: the Shopify App Store, other marketplaces (Google Workspace, Chrome, Slack, Atlassian, WordPress and others), and Google search for free tools and templates. The marketplaces win on one point that matters most for this founder: **the customer is already inside the store, searching for a fix, with a payment method on file**. Google search can work, but no one in the research found a payment-verified example of a small-business tool built on search traffic that also documented how long it took, and every "3–6 months to see search traffic" figure was a planning guess rather than measured data ([playbooks.com](https://playbooks.com/skills/skenetechnologies/plg-skills/free-tool-strategy)). Several small invoice tools on TrustMRR, a site that reads income straight from the founder's payment account, show zero to four paying customers ([TrustMRR InstantInvoice](https://trustmrr.com/startup/instantinvoice-io), [TrustMRR Trevidia](https://trustmrr.com/startup/trevidia-ltd?metric=mrr)).

**The Shopify App Store has the best terms of any channel studied.** Developers keep 100% of their first $1 million in lifetime app income (counted from January 1, 2025) and 85% after that, plus a 2.9% processing fee, and Shopify handles all billing ([Shopify docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share)). Shopify paid developers more than $1.3 billion last year, and installs grew nearly 20% year over year ([Shopify News](https://www.shopify.com/news/billion-dollar-ecosystem)). The proof that a dull utility can earn steadily there is Mergify, an app that merges a customer's separate orders into one shipment. A broker listing shows **$12,900 a month from 3,060 subscribers, with marketing limited to App Store ads and Google ads and under 20 hours a week of support** ([Flippa](https://flippa.com/12766656-mergify-is-a-shopify-app-that-combines-multiple-customer-orders-into-one-shipment-helping-stores-save-on-shipping-costs)). The founder of Zigpoll, a Shopify survey app run solo with no sales team, says the App Store brings about a third of new sign-ups but that traction took about two years ([Indie Hackers, search snippet only](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/c4o2Tfs6mjdpip5yZhaO)). The catch is a slow start: developers on Shopify's forum in 2026 reported that a new app with no reviews gets **0–2 installs a week** on its own, and several fell back on contacting shops to get their first ten users ([Shopify Community, search snippet only](https://community.shopify.com/t/how-did-you-get-your-first-10-organic-app-installations-in-2026/643974)). Shopify also has strict technical rules: billing must go through Shopify (no Stripe), and its official starter kit uses React Router rather than Next.js, though Supabase can still hold the data ([Shopify docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/shopify-app-store/app-store-requirements), [Shopify template](https://github.com/Shopify/shopify-app-template-react-router)).

**The Google Workspace Marketplace is the strongest second channel** because it shows public user counts, so demand is visible. Google takes no cut (the developer bills customers through Stripe, which fits the founder's current setup), although that "no cut" point was not re-checked against Google's own pages ([Vance Lucas](https://vancelucas.com/blog/how-i-built-a-google-sheets-extension-making-1-6k-mrr/)). Solo examples exist: Notion2Sheets reported about $8,400 a month from 400+ paying customers, and its users found it by searching the two product names in the store ([Medium/Indie Hustle](https://medium.com/indie-hustle/his-notion-micro-saas-business-makes-8-400-mrr-1e2869633ad9)). The trap is any add-on that reads Gmail or Drive content: Google then requires a yearly outside security audit estimated at $3,000–$15,000 ([Cadence](https://cadence.withremote.ai/blog/cost-to-build-google-workspace-addon), [Nango](https://nango.dev/docs/api-integrations/google-shared/google-security-review)). Add-ons that only touch Sheets or Calendar are believed to avoid that audit, which needs checking before building.

The other channels fit worse. Chrome extensions reach many people but earn little per user (one founder reported 500,000 users and $2,400 a month, from an anonymous, low-reliability source) ([NicheCheck](https://nichecheck.com/blog/chrome-extension-vs-saas)). Slack now throttles apps that are not approved for its directory ([Slack changelog](https://docs.slack.dev/changelog/2025/05/29/rate-limit-changes-for-non-marketplace-apps)). Microsoft's Teams store requires heavy sign-in and billing integration ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/marketplace-offers/plan-saas-offer)), and Wix takes 20% after year one ([Wix docs](https://dev.wix.com/docs/build-apps/launch-your-app/pricing-and-billing/about-monetizing-your-app)). WordPress plugins run on PHP rather than Next.js, and the business plugins checked there are mostly well rated and frequently updated ([WordPress.org API](https://api.wordpress.org/plugins/info/1.2/?action=query_plugins&request[search]=invoice)).

## Open gaps sit in back-office chores, not in sales widgets

Inside Shopify, star ratings hide the gaps, because almost every top app in every category is rated 4.8–5.0. The research found the gaps in three places instead: **free apps Shopify built itself that shops rate poorly, older apps whose recent 1–2 star reviews repeat the same failures, and small business-to-business niches where the top apps have under 200 reviews**. The categories to avoid are the ones that help shops sell more: product reviews, upsells, popups, back-in-stock alerts, pre-orders, delivery dates, size charts and cookie banners. In these, the top ten apps are almost all badge-holders with free plans and thousands of reviews. Judge.me alone has 49,179 reviews, and the top ten "upsell" apps hold 22,788 between them ([Shopify App Store search](https://apps.shopify.com/search?q=product+reviews)).

Shopify also places its own built-in features above apps for searches like "wholesale," "invoice," "purchase orders" and "metafields," so ideas that copy something Shopify already offers are risky ([Shopify App Store search "wholesale"](https://apps.shopify.com/search?q=wholesale)).

On Google Workspace, the opening is different: **add-ons with millions of users and poor ratings**. Three tools that turn spreadsheet or form rows into calendar events, Sheets2GCal (3.2★, 8M+ users), Calendar Events Creator (2.5★, 3M+) and Form to Calendar (3.4★, 1M+), together claim more than 12 million users ([Workspace Marketplace](https://workspace.google.com/marketplace/search/booking)). Calendar-to-timesheet tools look similar: myhours TimeSheet has 586K+ users at 4.0★, and TimeNavi has 19K+ at 3.2★ ([Workspace Marketplace](https://workspace.google.com/marketplace/search/timesheet)). User counts there are cumulative installs, not current users.

On Google search, the big free-tool searches are saturated or carry the wrong buyer. "Time card calculator" draws about 201,000 US searches a month, but mostly from workers checking their own hours. "Invoice generator" (about 49,500) is crowded with free tools and look-alikes ([seodata.dev](https://www.seodata.dev/keyword/time-card-calculator), [seodata.dev](https://www.seodata.dev/keyword/invoice-generator)). Pay stub generators have demand but a real fraud and legal problem; the FTC has sued a seller of fake pay stubs ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/cases/katrina_moore-_complaint.pdf)). The more promising search niches are mid-sized and specific to a trade, where advertisers pay a lot per click, a sign that the searchers buy software. Examples are "contractor estimate template" (1,600 searches a month, $13.63 a click) and "cleaning business software" (1,300 a month, $52.07 a click) ([seodata.dev](https://www.seodata.dev/keyword/contractor-estimate-template), [seodata.dev](https://www.seodata.dev/keyword/cleaning-business-software)).

Across all channels, payment-verified data shows what a steady small business tends to look like: **narrow jobs, a free tier, and years rather than months**. Web3Forms, a contact-form service run by one founder, now earns $43,387 a month from 3,149 subscriptions, but early on it had "20 paying customers in 20 months" ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/web3forms), [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/web3forms-breakdown)). EmailEngine, also run by one founder and listing search as its channel, earns $19,444 a month ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/emailengine)). Fast growth stories almost always involved paid ads, cold calling or a large social following. Pushouse, for example, lists cold calling and cold email among its channels ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/pushouse)). Crowded areas to avoid include AI writing tools, social media schedulers, AI search-ranking tools and generic chatbots ([TrustMRR social media](https://trustmrr.com/category/social-media)).

## The plan: a label app live by mid-January, judged on April 30

**Why the label app wins.** It is the only idea that combines everything this founder needs: complaints dated within the last six months against a big, weak incumbent; a store that brings customers and handles billing; a "documents out, nothing breaks" job that rarely creates urgent support; and a fallback that reuses the work. If labels do not take off, the same Shopify sign-in, billing and listing setup carries straight over to idea #2. The Google Sheets-to-Calendar idea has bigger raw numbers, but its users are a mix of individuals and businesses, it would be harder to build reliably, and the founder would carry Google's platform risk and set up billing alone. It is the best second bet if Shopify fails.

**What goes in the first version.** Pick products, variants or a collection, then print labels with any mix of product name, variant, price, compare-at price, SKU and barcode. Include fixed presets for common US label sheets, starting with Avery 5167 because a shop complained about losing exactly that, and common Dymo rolls, plus a custom-size option. Generate a print-ready PDF, save label designs so they never change after an update, and set quantity per variant (one label per unit in stock, or a set number). Billing through Shopify's own pricing tool: a free plan with a monthly label cap and one paid plan around $9.99–$14.99 a month. Add a short in-app guide on printing at "actual size," since alignment is where label tools fail.

**What stays out at first.** Leave out direct printer drivers, phone scanning, inventory counting, shelf-tag screens, other languages, AI features and anything that changes the shop's storefront. Printing labels for newly received purchase orders is the strongest feature to add second, but first confirm that Shopify lets apps read its built-in purchase orders. That was not checked.

**How to build and launch, in order.** Create a Shopify Partner account, pay the $19 listing fee ([Shopify docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share)), and start from Shopify's official React Router template rather than Next.js. Ask the AI coding assistant to keep Supabase as the database. Test on a free development store with real Avery sheets and a Dymo printer. Write the store listing around the exact words shops search ("price labels," "shelf labels," "barcode labels," "Avery 5167") and name the pains from the reviews: label sizes that never change, SKU on every label, and a human who answers. Record the required screencast and submit. After launch, answer every review and support email within a day, since slow, AI-only support is the most common complaint against rivals ([App Store, Event Tickets](https://apps.shopify.com/event-tickets), [App Store, Wholesale Gorilla](https://apps.shopify.com/wholesale-gorilla)). Buying a small amount of App Store search ads is allowed under the no-outreach rule and is how Mergify grew ([Flippa](https://flippa.com/12766656-mergify-is-a-shopify-app-that-combines-multiple-customer-orders-into-one-shipment-helping-stores-save-on-shipping-costs)). Once the app reaches 50 installs and 5 reviews, apply for the Built for Shopify badge.

**Go/no-go test.** The dates assume the build starts November 2, 2026. Shopify's review time was not found, so allow about a month for it.

| Date | Milestone | Pass | Fail and what to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dec 18, 2026 | App submitted to Shopify | Submitted, with the PDF printing correctly on real sheets | Cut scope to one sheet size and submit |
| ~Jan 15, 2027 | Live in the App Store | Listed | Fix whatever review issues Shopify raises |
| Feb 28, 2027 | Early check | 25+ installs and 3+ reviews | Under 10 installs: rewrite the listing title and description around different search words once |
| **Apr 30, 2027** | **Decision** | **60+ installs, 10+ paying shops, 5+ reviews averaging 4.5★ or higher** | **Under 20 installs: stop and move to the wholesale catalog maker, reusing the Shopify groundwork.** 20–60 installs but under 5 paying: adjust the free-plan cap or price once and recheck on Jun 30, 2027 |
| Oct 31, 2027 | One-year check | $500+ a month and growing | Flat: keep it running as-is and start idea #2 or #3 alongside it |

## How solid this evidence is, and what is still unknown

**What is first-hand.** Every Shopify app rating, review count, launch date, price and dated complaint was read directly from the live Shopify App Store on October 28, 2026. Google Workspace user counts, ratings and review quotes were read from that store on the same day. Shopify's revenue split, billing rules and badge rules come from Shopify's own developer documentation. The incomes for Web3Forms, EmailEngine, Conductor and the other TrustMRR products are read from the founders' payment accounts, though descriptions and channel claims on those pages are written by the founders. US search volumes come from Google Ads data via seodata.dev.

**What is estimated or second-hand.** All "time to $1,000 a month" and first-year income figures come from marketing blogs (Adsx, Convert2x, Week One Labs) with unknown methods, or from founder stories. The Mergify figures come from the seller's broker listing and are not audited. Churn figures are general industry averages, not data on these niches. The Google security-audit cost ranges from $3,000 to $75,000 depending on the source, and the high end comes from a vendor with a reason to inflate it. Every suggested price for the shortlisted products is a judgment, not a measured figure. Review counts stand in for installs because Shopify hides install numbers.

**What was blocked.** Indie Hackers and Medium refused access (error 403), Starter Story and some Google suggestion requests failed on a security-certificate error on this network, Similarweb was blocked, Dealslide rate-limited the requests, Acquire.com needed a login, Shopify's category pages would not load, and TrustMRR's full data needs a paid key. Anything cited from those sites comes from search-result previews only, and is labeled that way above.

**Where sources disagree.** "Time card calculator" shows 201,000 US searches a month in Google Ads but about 4,750 in Semrush. "Invoice generator" ranges from 49,500 to 318,100 depending on the tool. "Mileage log" disagrees between Google Ads and Google Trends by about 6 times. SignWell's revenue is reported as both $417K and $83K a month. Acquire.com still lists Shopify's take as 20%, which is out of date. Treat any single search volume as off by a factor of 2–3 in either direction.

**What to check before or during the build.** First, the paid prices and free-plan limits of the four strong barcode rivals, which were never collected and decide whether $9.99–$14.99 a month is realistic. Second, whether Shopify lets apps read its built-in purchase orders. Third, the share of label-app reviewers who are in the US (several quoted reviewers are not). Fourth, whether Shopify plans to fix or replace its own label app. Fifth, Shopify's current review time and required privacy hooks. For the Google ideas, confirm in Google's own documents that Calendar and Sheets access avoids the yearly security audit, and collect competitor prices.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which idea is biggest" to "where will a stranger find you while holding a credit card." For a founder who will not sell, that points to marketplaces, and specifically to the unglamorous corners of them, where the incumbent is the platform's own neglected free tool and buyers spell out in dated reviews what they want fixed. The size of a niche matters less than it seems. StoreMapper reportedly earns around $20,000 a month with only 97 reviews ([App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/storemapper), [Indie Hackers, search snippet only](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/qdReVAgLjz6EpW4OrJSI)), while the crowded categories reward funded teams instead.

The honest risk is not building the wrong feature but waiting too long in silence. Verified examples show steady income arrives over years, and new marketplace apps can sit at a few installs a week for months. A fixed decision date with a numeric bar, and a second idea that reuses the same groundwork, turns that slow start from an open-ended gamble into a bounded experiment the founder can afford to run.
