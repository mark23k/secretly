# Secret gifting, surprises, mutual crushes, and time-locked messages: US demand and competition for "Say Secretly"

Scope note: US focus, 2024-2026 where available. Older data is labeled with its year. Several key numbers come from company self-reports or third-party estimators (GetLatka, keyword tools, SpendHound); confidence is flagged inline. About 17 searches/fetches were run, so coverage is broad but shallow in places (see Gaps).

## Secret Santa / gift exchange: size, seasonality, competitors, monetization, complaints, office use

### Takeaway
This is a large but very seasonal category (Nov-Dec), dominated by free, entrenched incumbents (Elfster, DrawNames, Giftster) that make money from retail affiliate links and brand partnerships, not user fees. The paid gap is B2B: companies pay $69-$1,550+ per event for virtual White Elephant / gift-exchange experiences.

### Cited Findings
- Elfster: free to users; revenue comes from retailer affiliate links. In Oct 2021 it announced 25+ new retail partners, alongside existing merchants Amazon, Etsy, Fanatics, Nordstrom, Sephora, Zappos. Modern Retail framed its strategy as becoming an "alternative customer acquisition engine" for brands. — [Modern Retail, Nov 2021](https://www.modernretail.co/retailers/how-elfster-is-capitalizing-on-digital-gifting-to-entice-retail-brand-partners/); [Multichannel Merchant, 2021](https://multichannelmerchant.com/press-releases/elfster-adds-more-than-25-retail-partners-ahead-of-2021-holiday-season)
- Elfster scale: claims 17M+ users per year from 50+ countries (self-reported, 2021). Began as a social network in 2003. — [Modern Retail, 2021](https://www.modernretail.co/retailers/how-elfster-is-capitalizing-on-digital-gifting-to-entice-retail-brand-partners/)
- DrawNames: claims 40M+ users in 20+ countries over 25 years (cumulative, self-reported); homepage cites about 35M names drawn in 2024. — [DrawNames About](https://www.drawnames.com/about-us); [DrawNames homepage](https://www.drawnames.com:443)
- DrawNames shows up at about 3% adoption in Torii's Shadow IT Index (Dec 2025), meaning employees use it at work without IT signing off. This is an indirect sign of office use. — [Torii](https://www.toriihq.com/research/apps/drawnames)
- Giftster: free wish-list app on iOS, Android, web; supports Secret Santa exchanges. No paid tier found. — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/websites-apps/gifster); [GiftList comparison (competitor-authored)](https://giftlist.com/blog/giftster-vs-giftlist)
- Seasonality (third-party keyword estimates, not Google Trends): "secret santa gifts" at about 10.2K US monthly searches in Oct 2024, 48.9K in Nov 2024, 108.3K in Dec 2024, so roughly a 10x ramp from Oct to Dec. — [EcomCrew keyword tool](https://products.ecomcrew.com/search-term/secret%20santa%20gifts)
- Google said "Secret Santa" search interest rose 45% YoY in the MENA region (not US). This shows the term keeps growing, but it is not US data. — [Think with Google MENA](https://business.google.com/en-all/think/consumer-insights/holiday-season-retail-middle-east-and-north-africa/)
- Elfster complaints: email notifications keep arriving after users turn them off; users ask for better wish-list merging and cleanup tools (AI-aggregated review summary, low confidence). Post-update bugs left groups stuck on the loading screen. Support reviews are mixed. — [marlvel.ai summary](https://marlvel.ai/api/llm/apps/shopping/elfster-the-secret-santa-app); [JustUseApp reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/751594890/elfster-the-secret-santa-app/reviews); [ComplaintsBoard](https://www.complaintsboard.com/elfster-b127831)
- Corporate/remote White Elephant pricing:
  - CourseHorse live-hosted Zoom events: $750 for up to 10 people plus $75 per extra person (standard), or $1,550 plus $155 per person (premium). Other CourseHorse pages list $552 or $1,192 for up to 8 people, so the prices conflict between pages.
  - White Elephant Online (self-serve software): $69 for up to 10 players to $299 for up to 100 players, with business and personal licenses sold separately (may be outdated).
  - [CourseHorse](https://coursehorse.com/groups/virtual/event/online-white-elephant-gift-exchange); [SoftwareFinder: White Elephant Online](https://softwarefinder.com/hr/white-elephant-online); [The Go Game blog](https://thegogame.com/blog/virtual-white-elephant-gift-exchange)
- Many free single-purpose draw tools exist, e.g. Santasack.io, built "for fun, mostly" as a no-signup random assigner. Indie builders see this as a commodity. — [Wordshop / Santasack.io](https://wordshop.co.nz/?p=111651)

### Inferences
- A consumer Secret Santa drawer is commoditized: free incumbents with tens of millions of users, and SEO results crowded by DrawNames and Elfster. A solo founder is unlikely to win on name drawing alone. Monetization would need affiliate volume, which in turn needs scale.
- The most winnable angle is a paid B2B product that is also the "secret" part of the brand: a self-serve office gift exchange or virtual White Elephant ($69-$299 per event per White Elephant Online) handling remote shipping, budget rules, and anonymity. That sits between the free tools and $750+ hosted events.
- Seasonality means about 6-8 weeks of revenue per year unless the product also covers birthdays, Valentine's, and office "Secret Pal" programs.

### Gaps
- Exact Google Trends curves for "secret santa" / "white elephant" (US) were not retrieved; only third-party keyword-volume estimates.
- No revenue figures for Elfster, DrawNames, Giftster, or SecretSanta.com. SecretSanta.com's model was not found.
- No Reddit (r/secretsanta, r/AskHR, r/remotework) threads were surfaced by search; complaint data is thin and partly AI-aggregated.
- No 2024-2026 Elfster user count; the 17M figure is from 2021.

## Surprise coordination: group cards/video tributes, group gift collection, surprise parties

### Takeaway
Group appreciation boards and video montages are a proven paid category with both per-event B2C pricing ($7-$99) and B2B SaaS subscriptions ($299-$449/yr+). Tribute.co, which is bootstrapped, reportedly reached about $3.2M revenue in 2024. Group money collection for gifts has had no clear standalone winner since Tilt shut down in 2017.

### Cited Findings
- Kudoboard pricing (fetched 2026), per board:
  - Lite: $6.99 (20 posts, can attach a gift card)
  - Premium: $9.99 (100 posts, video)
  - Milestone: $19.99 (unlimited posts)
  - Memorial: $99
  - 5/10/25-board pack: $35/yr
- Kudoboard subscriptions:
  - Business: $25/mo, or $299/yr billed annually
  - Pro: $38/mo, or $449/yr billed annually
  - Enterprise (501+ employees): custom pricing
  - Company Event: contact sales
- Kudoboard says it helps "millions of people" and shows logos including T-Mobile, Gusto, Coca-Cola, Atlassian, and Airbnb. Gift cards can be collected from the board creator or from everyone. — [Kudoboard pricing](https://www.kudoboard.com/pricing)
- Kudoboard, Inc., San Francisco, was founded in 2013 and is private. SpendHound (self-reported contract data from 160 customers, unverified) puts average spend at about $126/yr for SMB and $180/yr for enterprise. Reviewers say it gets expensive for large teams. — [Fitgap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/kudoboard); [SpendHound](https://www.spendhound.com/marketplace/kudoboard-pricing); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/182991/Kudoboard/pricing/)
- Tribute.co (group video montages): GetLatka estimates revenue at $2.1M (2023) and $3.2M (2024) and calls the company bootstrapped. CB Insights lists $4M raised, which conflicts with "bootstrapped". Treat both as estimates. Pricing is about $29.99 self-serve and $99 done-for-you; AlternativeTo lists one-time prices of $29-$99 plus a limited free version. — [GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/tributeco); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/tributeco); [Kristen Berman teardown](https://kristenberman.substack.com/p/the-science-of-how-tribute-gets-you); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/tribute/about)
- VidHug (group video) was acquired by Punchbowl (online invitations) for an undisclosed price. Punchbowl folded VidHug's technology into its "Memento" brand and is retiring the VidHug name. The search summary ties a $5M SG Credit Partners financing to the deal. — [TechAlliance](https://techalliance.ca/?p=1110); [VidDay comparison](https://www.vidday.com/best-group-video-maker-comparison)
- Tilt (group money pooling for trips, parties, gifts; its key feature was not charging anyone until a group threshold was met) was acquired by Airbnb in early 2017. Its platform was retired in June 2017. — [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/paytech/payments-firm-tilt-wilts); [Civic Tech Guide](https://directory.civictech.guide/listing/tilt); [TechCrunch (older)](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1049603)

### Inferences
- "Surprise for someone" (birthday, farewell, wedding, retirement) is a natural fit for "Say Secretly" and evergreen: it happens all year, unlike Secret Santa. The playbook is proven: free to start, then charge per board or video at $7-$30, then upsell teams to an annual subscription. Tribute's estimated $3M+ shows a small team can build a multi-million business here.
- The main differentiator still open is secrecy: keeping the recipient from finding out, managing invite links, timing the reveal, plus pooled gift money. A solo founder would need Stripe Connect or a gift-card API, which adds payments and KYC complexity.
- The B2B side (HR, team leads doing farewells and work anniversaries) pays more reliably than consumers. Kudoboard's subscriptions show this, but it also means competing with an established incumbent.

### Gaps
- GroupGreeting pricing page returned 403; no GroupGreeting pricing or revenue was captured.
- No data on Givingli, Kudoboard revenue or headcount, or current (2024-2026) group gift-pooling apps. A search for Tilt replacements returned only 2016-2017 results.
- No surprise-party planning tool data was collected (e.g., Punchbowl, Evite, Partiful); not researched.

## Mutual-crush / secret admirer matching ("only tells you if it's mutual") and anonymous compliments

### Takeaway
Demand is real but comes in viral bursts, mostly among students. It peaks around Valentine's Day and campus events, and products tend to spike then die. Monetization is weak except through big-platform acquisition or teen subscription gimmicks.

### Cited Findings
- Marriage Pact (campus compatibility matcher, platform-run): used on 109 campuses since 2017, with 314,488 matches (as of Nov 2025). Stanford's 2025 pact had 4,177 participants. Brown reported 3,158 sign-ups in its first year (2025), rising to 4,177. That second figure matches Stanford's exactly, so one outlet may have it wrong. Middlebury: 1,511 participants, 625 romantic and 130 platonic matches (date unclear). Tufts (2020): about half of undergraduates took part, and roughly 3,000 received matches. — [Baylor Lariat, Nov 2025](https://baylorlariat.com/2025/11/12/baylor-marriage-pact-finds-students-a-perfect-match/); [Stanford Daily, May 2026](https://stanforddaily.com/2026/05/05/marriage-pact-campus-report-reveals-the-years-dating-trends/); [Brown Daily Herald, Mar 2026](https://www.browndailyherald.com/article/2026/03/how-students-found-lasting-love-through-marriage-pact); [Middlebury Campus](https://middleburycampus.com/54231/news/a-match-made-in-algorithm-60-of-students-participate-in-the-middlebury-marriage-pact/); [Tufts Daily, 2020](https://tuftsdaily.com/article/2020/12/half-of-undergraduate-student-body-completes-marriage-pact-almost-3000-receive-matches); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage_Pact)
- Facebook Dating "Secret Crush" (launched 2019): you pick a limited number of friends, and they are never told unless they pick you too. — [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/atom/facebook-take-tinder-hinge-tests-secret-crushes-feature-101201); [Santa Fe New Mexican](https://www.santafenewmexican.com/news/would-you-confess-your-secret-crush-to-facebook/article_018daf5b-ebdb-534b-81da-4f8e7628e84d.html)
- Current indie mutual-crush apps:
  - "Mutual" (iOS): anonymous notifications to people picked from your contacts; identities revealed only if feelings are mutual.
  - MetLL (Product Hunt launch): reveals a match only when the feeling is mutual.
  - Scrush and Princeton's TigerCrush (2020): TigerCrush also shows a count of unreciprocated "secret admirers".
  - [App Store: Mutual](https://apps.apple.com/ph/app/mutual/id6744620671); [Product Hunt: MetLL](https://www.producthunt.com/products/metll); [Daily Princetonian, 2020](https://dailyprincetonian.com/article/2020/11/isolated-students-turn-to-tiger-crush); [Her Campus](https://www.hercampus.com/?p=601254)
- Anonymous compliments:
  - Gas (by Nikita Bier) launched Aug 2022 and hit 1M daily active users within two months, US/Canada iOS only.
  - Discord acquired Gas in Jan 2023 (undisclosed price) and shut it down on Nov 7, 2023.
  - Bier's earlier app, tbh, was sold to Facebook in 2017 and shut down in 2018 for low usage.
  - [TechCrunch, Jan 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/01/17/discord-acquires-gas-a-compliments-based-social-media-app-for-teens); [TechCrunch, Oct 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/discord-kills-gas-anonymous-compliments-app-bought-nine-months-ago); [Wikipedia: Gas](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_(app))

### Inferences
- Mutual-crush matching fits "Say Secretly" best on brand, but it is a viral-loop, network-effect product. It needs both people on the platform, works best inside a closed group (campus, school, office), and fades fast once the novelty wears off (Gas, tbh).
- For a solo founder, a lightweight, shareable Valentine's mechanic is more realistic than a standalone app: "enter your crush's phone or email; we only tell you both if it's mutual". Treat it as a seasonal acquisition hook (Feb spike) feeding evergreen products, not as the core business.
- Risks: anonymous messaging to teens draws moderation, bullying, and safety scrutiny. Contact-based outreach also raises spam and SMS compliance issues (TCPA in the US).

### Gaps
- No revenue data for Marriage Pact, Mutual, MetLL, or Gas's paid tier. No App Store download data.
- Datamatch (Harvard-born Valentine's matcher) participation numbers were not retrieved.
- Duolicious and Hinge crush-style features were not researched.

## Delayed / time-capsule secret messages (FutureMe, wedding/birthday video messages)

### Takeaway
FutureMe is the clearest solo-founder success in the "secret" set. One founder ran it on about $500/month in costs for about 19 years and sold it in July 2021 for a reported "mid-7 figures". Demand is evergreen, with New Year's, graduation, and viral spikes.

### Cited Findings
- FutureMe:
  - Founded 2002 by Matt Sly; sold July 2021 to Memories Group Ltd (digital legacy/reflection software) for a reported "mid-7 figures".
  - "Many millions of users" over its life and "millions of users per year".
  - Three revenue lines: consumer payments, advertising, and SaaS custom sites.
  - Operating costs about $500/mo with one part-time contractor; the first version ran on an $11/mo shared server.
  - About six major viral moments: Yahoo homepage (2004), AP (2006), TikTok during the pandemic.
  - [TheyGotAcquired, Aug 2023](https://theygotacquired.com/software/futureme-acquired-by-memories-group/)
- FutureMe pricing (PCWorld, date unclear): about $2/yr to send a message to any future date, or $12/yr for unlimited messages. Early on it relied on donations, about $58 total in a 2007-era report. A Forbes.com "email time capsule" promotion collected 140K+ letters in about 6 weeks (older). — [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/3072843/send-a-letter-to-your-future-self-with-futureme.html); [NPR, 2007](https://www.npr.org/2007/03/31/9261640/futureme-org-takes-users-into-the-future); [NBC News (older)](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna10530680)
- Event video messages: Tribute.co ($29-$99 per video, about $3.2M est. 2024 revenue) and Punchbowl's Memento (formerly VidHug) serve the wedding, birthday, and retirement "surprise video" use case. — [GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/tributeco); [TechAlliance](https://techalliance.ca/?p=1110)

### Inferences
- "Write it now, they see it later" (letters to a future self, to a child at 18, or wedding guests' messages opened on an anniversary) is evergreen, cheap to run, needs no network effect, and relies heavily on SEO and viral spikes. That makes it a good solo-founder fit. FutureMe proves an exit is possible but also holds the main SEO term.
- A differentiated angle: time-locked messages to other people (not only to yourself), group time capsules for weddings, graduations, and a baby's first year, and "reveal on a date" surprise collections. These fit "Say Secretly" and overlap with the group-card category, where people already pay $10-$30 per event.

### Gaps
- No current FutureMe user, letter, or revenue figures, and current pricing was not verified on its site.
- No data on other time-capsule competitors (e.g., wedding time-capsule apps, "letters to my kid" apps).

## Evergreen vs. seasonal; B2B vs. B2C; evidence of indie/solo founder revenue

### Takeaway
Evergreen plus monetizable: group cards and surprise video tributes (B2C and B2B) and time-locked messages (B2C). Spiky: Secret Santa and White Elephant (Nov-Dec, free consumer tools; paid B2B events) and mutual-crush (Feb and campus terms; viral, hard to monetize). The strongest solo-founder evidence is FutureMe (solo, mid-7-figure exit) and Tribute.co (reportedly bootstrapped, about $3M+).

### Cited Findings
- Seasonal spike: "secret santa gifts" searches run about 10x higher in Dec than Oct (2024 estimates). — [EcomCrew](https://products.ecomcrew.com/search-term/secret%20santa%20gifts)
- B2B willingness to pay:
  - Kudoboard: $299-$449/yr subscriptions plus enterprise and company-event plans. — [Kudoboard pricing](https://www.kudoboard.com/pricing)
  - Virtual White Elephant: $69-$299 self-serve, $552-$1,550+ hosted. — [SoftwareFinder](https://softwarefinder.com/hr/white-elephant-online); [CourseHorse](https://coursehorse.com/groups/virtual/event/online-white-elephant-gift-exchange)
- Solo or small-team outcomes:
  - FutureMe: solo founder, about $500/mo costs, mid-7-figure exit (2021). — [TheyGotAcquired](https://theygotacquired.com/software/futureme-acquired-by-memories-group/)
  - Tribute.co: est. $3.2M revenue (2024), reportedly bootstrapped (conflicts with CB Insights' $4M raised). — [GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/tributeco); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/tributeco)
- Consumer viral/social tools ended in acquire-and-shut-down outcomes rather than sustained revenue (tbh in 2017-18, Gas in 2023, Tilt in 2017). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/discord-kills-gas-anonymous-compliments-app-bought-nine-months-ago); [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/paytech/payments-firm-tilt-wilts)
- No Indie Hackers revenue reports for Secret Santa tools were found; free hobby generators are common. — [Wordshop / Santasack.io](https://wordshop.co.nz/?p=111651)

### Inferences
- Ranking for a solo founder on saysecretly.com, from my synthesis:
  1. Surprise group card / video / message collection with a timed reveal (evergreen, per-event payment, B2B upsell). This is the best mix of demand, willingness to pay, and brand fit.
  2. Time-locked "say it later" messages (evergreen, low cost, SEO and viral driven; FutureMe precedent).
  3. Office Secret Santa / White Elephant as a seasonal B2B product (paid $69-$299 tier). Good Q4 cash, but competes with free incumbents for consumers.
  4. Mutual-crush matcher as a Valentine's viral hook only.
- One "secret" platform could cover all of these (collect secretly, reveal later), so the seasonal products bring in traffic for the evergreen ones.

### Gaps
- No primary revenue disclosures from any indie in these niches beyond FutureMe's exit range. No Product Hunt traction metrics were gathered.
- Search demand for evergreen terms ("group card", "time capsule letter", "future letter") was not quantified.
